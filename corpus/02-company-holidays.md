## 1. Purpose

Harborline closes its US offices and pauses non-critical operations on designated company holidays so employees can observe civic and cultural days without using PTO. This policy lists the 2026 US holiday set, the two floating holidays, weekend-observance rules, the Ireland calendar, and what happens when someone must work.

A holiday is a **paid day off**. It is not deducted from PTO. If you are already on a leave of absence, holiday pay follows the leave rules in **POL-LOA-009** rather than this policy.

## 3. Floating holidays

Every regular employee on US payroll receives **two floating holidays** (16 hours) each calendar year. Floating holidays are meant for religious observances, cultural days, or personal dates that are not on the company list.

Rules:

- Granted on 1 January, or prorated at 8 hours if you start on or after 1 July.
- Request them in HarborHub as **Floating Holiday**, not PTO.
- Manager approval uses the same notice table as PTO in **POL-PTO-001**.
- Unused floating holidays expire at 11:59 p.m. Pacific Time on 31 December. They do **not** carry over and are **not** paid out at termination.
- You may not take a floating holiday on a day that is already a company holiday.
- You may take floating holidays in 8-hour or 4-hour blocks only (no 1-hour floating holidays).

Examples of appropriate use: Yom Kippur, Diwali, Eid, Lunar New Year, a day of rest your religion requires, or the birthday of a child you co-parent on an off-schedule year. You do not have to disclose the religious or personal reason beyond a short note that the day is a floating holiday.

## 4. Weekend and mid-week observance rules

When a listed holiday falls on Saturday, Harborline observes the preceding Friday. When it falls on Sunday, Harborline observes the following Monday. The 2026 Independence Day line in Section 2 is an example.

If two holidays would collapse onto the same observed weekday (rare), People Operations will publish a substitute day within 30 days of the calendar year start. That announcement appears in Slack #harbor-people.

Employees who work a non-Monday–Friday pattern (for example, a support engineer whose weekend is Tuesday–Wednesday) receive holiday pay on the **observed** date if it was a scheduled workday, or a substitute day in the same pay period if the observed date was already their weekend. Coordinate the substitute day with your manager in HarborHub as **Holiday — Alternate**.

Irish employees receive statutory public-holiday entitlements plus company closure on the days above. They receive **one** company floating holiday (8 hours) rather than two, because the Irish public-holiday count is already comparable to the US set.

Employees who temporarily travel from Dublin to Seattle (or the reverse) keep their **home payroll** holiday calendar. Do not expect the destination office to be closed on your holiday. If you must work a home-calendar holiday while on site in another country, use Section 7.

Harborline does not currently employ people on UK, German, or other country payrolls. If you will work from another country for more than 10 business days, **POL-RMT-003** requires a written exception. Holiday treatment is part of that exception.

## 6. Eligibility for holiday pay

You receive holiday pay if you are a regular employee or intern in active status on the observed date. Active status includes PTO, floating holiday, and approved sick days immediately before or after the holiday.

You do **not** receive an extra holiday day if you are on unpaid leave, on a leave that already pays you at 100% (parental leave, company-paid medical leave), or if your last day of employment was before the observed date.

Part-time employees receive holiday pay only when the holiday falls on a day they were scheduled to work. Pay equals the hours they would have worked that day, up to 8.

Non-exempt employees who are authorized to work on an observed holiday are paid their regular rate for hours worked **plus** 8 hours of holiday pay (or scheduled hours if part-time). Overtime, if any, is calculated on hours actually worked, not on the holiday add-on. Exempt employees who must work a holiday receive a substitute day off within 30 days, scheduled with their manager.

## 7. Working on a holiday

Most teams do not work holidays. Exceptions: customer production incidents, on-call rotations, and pre-approved cutovers.

To schedule holiday work:

1. The manager writes a HarborHub case at least 14 days in advance (incidents are same-day).
2. The employee consents. Holiday work is not mandatory except for documented on-call rotations in the job description.
3. Pay or substitute time is applied as in Section 6.
4. Customer Success and Engineering on-call calendars must show coverage by 1 December for the following year’s Thanksgiving and Christmas window.

On-call employees who are **not** called do not receive extra holiday pay; they already receive on-call premium under their team’s compensation memo.

## 8. Offices, Slack, and customer communications

On US company holidays:

- Seattle and Austin offices are locked except for badge-authorized incident responders.
- Slack remains available. #harbor-incidents is staffed by the on-call rotation only.
- External auto-replies should state the office is closed and give the return date plus the incident email (support@harborline.example).
- Customer contracts that promise 24/7 support are covered by the on-call runbook, not by asking random employees to “just hop on.”

The day after Thanksgiving (27 November 2026) is a full company holiday, not a half day. Christmas Eve 2026 is a full holiday. There is no half-day schedule in 2026.

## 9. Religious accommodations beyond floating holidays

Two floating holidays will not cover every religious calendar. If you need additional days, request PTO first. If PTO is exhausted or the dates conflict with a blackout, email hr@harborline.example to request a religious accommodation under the handbook. Accommodations may include unpaid time, a shift swap, or remote coverage. Harborline will not ask you to prove your faith with clergy letters as a default; we may ask clarifying questions about schedule impact.

## 10. Interaction with PTO, travel, and remote work

- PTO that overlaps a holiday is split: holiday hours use the Holiday code. See **POL-PTO-001** Section 10.
- Travel days adjacent to a holiday are workdays unless you take PTO. A Friday flight home after a Thursday customer meeting is work time if you are working; it is not automatically a holiday.
- Remote employees do not receive extra days because their school district or state government is closed. Use PTO or a floating holiday for those closures.
- If you relocate mid-year from the US to Ireland (or the reverse), People Operations converts you to the destination calendar on the payroll transfer date. Unused US floating holidays are forfeited at transfer; they are not cashed out.

## 11. Publishing the next year’s calendar

People Operations publishes the next calendar-year holiday list by **1 October**. Until then, treat the current year’s list plus the weekend-observance rule as the planning guide. The HTML companion file `holiday-calendar-2026.html` is the printable view of this policy’s dates.

## 12. Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is New Year’s Eve 2026 a holiday?**  
A. No. 31 December 2026 is a workday. Use PTO or a floating holiday if you want it off.

**Q. Do contractors get holiday pay?**  
A. Agency contractors follow their agency agreement. Harborline offices are closed; they should not be asked to sit in an empty office.

**Q. I am on parental leave on Thanksgiving. Do I get an extra day later?**  
A. No. Company-paid parental leave already pays that week at 100%. See **POL-FAM-011**.

**Q. Can I save both floating holidays and use them in the last week of December?**  
A. Yes, if your manager approves and the days are not already company holidays. They still expire if unused.

**Q. The Austin Independent School District is closed for a staff day. Is Harborline closed?**  
A. No. Use PTO, sick time (if a child is ill), or remote-work flexibility in **POL-RMT-003**.
