# Leaves of Absence Policy

**Policy ID:** POL-LOA-009  
**Owner:** People Operations — Leave Administration  
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026  
**Last revised:** 1 September 2026  
**Applies to:** Regular employees; statutory rights also apply to others as required by law  
**Related:** POL-HB-000, POL-PTO-001, POL-HOL-002, POL-BEN-006, POL-FAM-011, POL-CON-010

---

## 1. Purpose

Sometimes you need more than a PTO day. This policy covers medical leave, US Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA) leave, state paid-family programs, bereavement, jury and witness duty, military leave, personal unpaid leave, and sabbatical. Parental and caregiver leave have their own policy (**POL-FAM-011**) because employees ask those questions as a set. The two documents are meant to be read together when a birth, adoption, or family medical event is involved.

Leave administration is not your manager’s side project. Email hr@harborline.example with the subject line **LOA** and the dates you know. We will tell you which forms to file and whether to involve the disability carrier.

## 2. How to request any leave of 4 or more consecutive workdays

1. Tell your manager the expected dates at a high level. You do not have to disclose a diagnosis.
2. Email hr@harborline.example the same day. Include start date, expected end date, whether the leave is medical or family, and whether you have already seen a provider.
3. People Operations sends the packet (FMLA notice, medical certification, disability forms, state PFML instructions) within **2 business days**.
4. Return medical certification within **15 calendar days** unless we grant more time.
5. Do not wait until you have exhausted PTO. Protected leave and PTO are different.

Absences of 1–3 days for ordinary illness use sick time or PTO (**POL-PTO-001**). After **3 consecutive workdays** of illness, your manager notifies People Operations even if you feel you will be back tomorrow.

## 3. US FMLA (unpaid, job-protected)

Eligible US employees may take up to **12 weeks** of unpaid FMLA leave in a rolling 12-month period measured backward from the date leave is used, for:

- Your own serious health condition
- Care of a spouse, child, or parent with a serious health condition
- Bonding with a child after birth, adoption, or foster placement (also see **POL-FAM-011**)
- Qualifying military exigency

Eligible employees may take up to **26 weeks** to care for a covered servicemember in a single 12-month period.

**Eligibility:** 12 months of employment and 1,250 hours worked in the 12 months before leave starts, and employment at a location where 50 or more employees work within 75 miles — or as the statute otherwise provides. Harborline has 50+ employees in the Seattle area; remote employees are counted with their assigned hub or, if fully remote, as the law requires. If you are not FMLA-eligible, you may still qualify for Company medical leave, state leave, or an accommodation.

FMLA can be continuous or intermittent (for example, migraines or physical therapy). Intermittent leave needs a certification that states probable frequency. Calendar it honestly.

Harborline continues US medical benefits during FMLA on the same cost-share as if you were working. You must pay your premium share; we will arrange catch-up deductions or invoices. If you do not return, we may recover Company-paid premiums as the FMLA allows.

FMLA is unpaid. You may elect to use PTO or sick time to remain in pay status, except that Company-paid parental leave already pays 100% for the weeks in **POL-FAM-011** and should not be double-dipped.

## 4. Company medical leave and short-term disability

US employees with a serious health condition who are not yet FMLA-eligible, or who need time beyond a waiting period, may receive **Company medical leave** of up to 12 weeks in a rolling 12 months, running **concurrently** with FMLA and STD when those apply.

Pay during Company medical leave:

- Days 1–7: sick time, then PTO, then unpaid.
- After day 7: short-term disability at **60%** of base pay (**POL-BEN-006**).
- Harborline **tops up STD to 80%** of base pay for up to 8 weeks for regular employees with at least 1 year of service. Top-up is Company policy, not an insurance right.

Fitness-for-duty notes are required when the certification says so, and whenever the leave exceeded 10 workdays for a physical condition that affects safety-sensitive work (warehouse visits, driving, production on-call).

Ireland occupational sick pay is described in the Irish contract and is at least the statutory sick-pay scheme. Dublin employees contact hr@harborline.example; do not file a US STD claim.

## 5. State paid family and medical leave

Washington PFML, California PFL/CFRA, New York DBL/PFL, Colorado FAMLI, and similar programs may pay you a partial wage. Harborline requires you to **apply** when People Operations says you are in a covered state. Company pay (parental 100%, medical top-up, PTO) is coordinated so total replacement does not exceed 100% of base pay. Overpayments must be returned.

These state programs can have different eligibility than FMLA (sometimes fewer hours). Ask. Do not assume a denial from the state means you have no Company leave.

## 6. Bereavement

Paid bereavement leave:

| Relationship | Paid days |
| --- | --- |
| Spouse or domestic partner, child, stepchild, parent, stepparent, sibling, grandchild, grandparent, or someone for whom you were the primary caregiver | **5** workdays |
| Parent-in-law, sibling-in-law, or a person who lived in your household | **3** workdays |
| Other relative (aunt, uncle, cousin) or a close friend | **1** workday |

Days need not be consecutive but should be taken within 30 days of the death unless a delayed memorial requires a HarborHub note. You may add PTO. We may ask for an obituary or death certificate if the dates are extended or the relationship is unclear; we do not do this by default for immediate family.

Travel that needs more than the paid days is PTO or unpaid personal leave.

## 7. Jury duty, witness duty, and voting

Harborline pays your regular base pay for jury duty for up to **10 workdays** per year. Beyond 10 days, pay is unpaid unless state law requires more; you may use PTO. Send the summons to hr@harborline.example. You keep any small statutory jury fee.

Witness duty in a Harborline-related matter is paid as work time. Witness duty in a personal case is PTO or unpaid.

Voting: if your work schedule does not allow a reasonable time to vote, you will receive up to two paid hours at the start or end of the day, consistent with state law. Ask your manager the day before.

## 8. Military leave

Harborline follows USERRA (US) and Irish Defence Forces protections. Military leave is unpaid except:

- The first **10 workdays** of annual training or drill in a calendar year are paid at full base (you offset any military pay if the finance process asks; many employees simply keep both for short drills — Payroll will state the current practice in the leave letter).
- Differential pay may be approved by the Chief People Officer for longer deployments.

Reinstatement, benefits, and seniority follow USERRA. Provide orders when you can. Emergency activation can be notice-after-the-fact.

## 9. Personal unpaid leave

When no statute or Company program fits, you may request **personal unpaid leave** of up to 30 calendar days after 1 year of service. Approval is discretionary (workload, prior attendance, reason). Benefits continuation beyond the month you leave unpaid status may require COBRA. You cannot stack personal leave to extend a denied medical certification.

Personal leave is not a way to take a remote month in another country. That is **POL-RMT-003**.

## 10. Sabbatical

Regular employees who have completed **7 years** of continuous service may request a sabbatical of **4 unpaid weeks** (20 workdays) with benefits remaining on the same cost-share. One sabbatical per 7-year block. Apply 90 days ahead. You may use PTO to remain in pay for some or all of the four weeks. Sabbatical cannot be taken during a documented blackout (**POL-PTO-001**) without function-VP approval.

Sabbatical is not severance and does not change at-will status.

## 11. Job protection, performance, and secondary employment

Job protection exists where the law or this policy says so (FMLA, USERRA, Company parental leave, Company medical leave for the stated duration). Personal unpaid leave is not job-protected in the same way; we will try to hold the role but may fill it if the business requires.

You may not work a second job that conflicts with a medical certification (“totally disabled”) without telling Leave Administration. Fraudulent claims are a conduct issue.

Performance processes pause for protected leave; they may resume when you return. Leave is not a shield for misconduct that already occurred.

## 12. Holidays and PTO during leave

If a Company holiday falls during fully paid Company leave (parental, bereavement, paid jury), you do not receive an extra floating day later. If a holiday falls during unpaid FMLA, you do not receive holiday pay unless you were in a paid status that week. See **POL-HOL-002**.

Accrual of PTO continues while you are in paid status and pauses during unpaid leave of more than 10 workdays, except where state law requires continued vacation accrual.

## 13. Return to work

People Operations schedules a return-to-work conversation. IT confirms that access still makes sense. If you need a reduced schedule or equipment change, that is an accommodation request, not a silent experiment.

Failure to return on the agreed date without contact is handled as a no-call / no-show (**POL-PTO-001**).

## 14. Frequently asked questions

**Q. Do I have to use all my PTO before FMLA starts?**  
A. No. You may elect to use PTO to stay paid. We will not force you to drain the bank before a medical leave, except where a state statute lets an employer require substitution — and even then we will say so in your leave letter.

**Q. I need Thursday mornings off for physical therapy for six weeks. Is that leave?**  
A. Yes, intermittent FMLA or Company medical if the condition qualifies. Put it on the calendar and have your provider complete the form.

**Q. My father-in-law died. How many days do I get?**  
A. Three paid days (Section 6).

**Q. Does short-term disability pay 100%?**  
A. No. STD is 60%. Company top-up can bring you to 80% for 8 weeks if you have a year of service. Parental leave is the 100% program.

**Q. I am in Ireland. Do I file FMLA?**  
A. No. FMLA is a US statute. Use your Irish statutory leave plus **POL-FAM-011** / this policy’s Irish notes.
