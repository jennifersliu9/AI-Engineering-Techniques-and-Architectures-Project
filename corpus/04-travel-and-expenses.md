# Travel and Expense Policy

**Policy ID:** POL-EXP-004  
**Owner:** Finance Operations  
**Effective date:** 1 January 2026  
**Last revised:** 1 September 2026  
**Applies to:** All employees, interns, and others who spend Harborline funds  
**Related:** POL-HB-000, POL-RMT-003, POL-EQP-008, POL-CON-010  
**Companion limits sheet:** expense-limits-2026.pdf

---

## 1. Purpose

Harborline will pay for reasonable, necessary, and documented costs of doing Company business. This policy tells you how to book travel, what meals and hotels may cost, when you need a receipt, how corporate cards work, and what we will not reimburse.

The one-page limits in `expense-limits-2026.pdf` match the tables below. If a manager quotes a different number in Slack, this policy wins unless the VP of Finance approved an exception in HarborHub.

Spending Company money is not a benefit. Inflating expenses, splitting receipts to dodge a threshold, or booking personal travel in a way that raises the Company fare is a conduct issue under **POL-CON-010**.

## 2. Systems and who approves

| Action | System | Approver |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Book flight, hotel, rail, or car | HarborTravel | Manager (auto if fare is within policy) |
| Submit out-of-pocket expenses | Voyage | Manager, then Finance operations |
| Corporate card transactions | Voyage (auto-imported) | Cardholder codes them; manager approves the report |
| Exception above a published cap | HarborHub case | VP of Finance; CFO if over $2,500 |

Submit Voyage reports within **15 calendar days** of the expense (or of the return date for a trip). Reports older than 60 days need Director-level approval and may be denied.

Harborline’s tax year is the calendar year. Expenses from late December must still be in Voyage by 15 January so Finance can close the books.

## 3. When travel is allowed

Book travel only when the meeting cannot achieve the same outcome on a video call, or when a contract, implementation, or incident requires a person on site.

Required approvals before you click buy:

- In-country travel under $1,500 total estimated cost: manager.
- In-country travel $1,500–$5,000: manager and director.
- International travel, or any trip over $5,000: manager, director, and VP of the function.
- Conference travel: also needs a learning-budget code from People Operations if you will attend sessions (not just staff a booth).

Sales and Customer Success have standing authority to visit named accounts in their territory. They still book through HarborTravel so security and duty-of-care tools can see the itinerary.

## 4. Booking rules (air, rail, car)

**Air.** Book the lowest logical economy fare that gets you there within two hours of the requested arrival. HarborTravel’s “policy fare” flag is the default. Upgrades:

- Paid extra-legroom seats: allowed on flights of 4 hours or more, up to $75 each way.
- Premium economy: allowed on flights of 6 hours or more with director approval.
- Business class: CFO approval only; typically reserved for 10+ hour flights or a documented medical need.
- Preferred-airline status you earned personally may be used. Do not add a fare to collect points.

Book at least **14 days** in advance for domestic and **21 days** for international. Inside those windows, a higher fare is not an exception if you simply booked late without a customer-driven reason.

**Rail.** Preferred for Boston–New York–Washington, Dublin–other Irish cities, and Seattle–Portland when the rail door-to-door time is within 90 minutes of flying.

**Rental cars.** Midsize or smaller. Skip collision-damage waiver if you use the corporate card (the card provides primary CDW in the US). Take the waiver outside the US. Do not rent luxury, sports, or 12-passenger vans without Safety and Finance approval. Personal use of a rental during a trip is allowed for evenings and one weekend day on a multi-week on-site; fuel for personal side trips is not reimbursed.

**Rideshare and transit.** Allowed for airport and local trips. Keep the e-receipt. Airport lounge day-passes are reimbursable only on a delay of 2 hours or more when Harborline caused the connection.

**Personal car.** Mileage is reimbursed at the current IRS standard rate (US) or Revenue Commissioners civil-service rate (Ireland). HarborHub will show the rate Finance loaded for the year. You may not claim both mileage and fuel. Commuting between your home and your assigned hub is never mileage-eligible (**POL-RMT-003**).

## 5. Hotels and lodging

| Location | Nightly cap (room + tax target) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US cities generally | **$225** | Book HarborTravel negotiated rates first |
| New York City, San Francisco, Washington DC, Boston | **$325** | Still economy/business hotels, not luxury |
| International generally | **$280** | Local currency equivalent |
| London, Zurich, Tokyo, Singapore | **$380** | Pre-approval if over |

Caps are for the room rate including mandatory taxes and resort fees. Breakfast included in the room rate does not reduce your meal cap, but do not also expense a hotel breakfast buffet if it was already in the room rate.

Airbnbs and short-term apartments are allowed for stays of 7 nights or more when cheaper than a hotel and when Information Security has no objection (private room, lockable door, you are not sharing with strangers). They are not allowed for one-night stays.

Cancellation: use refundable or moderately flexible rates when the meeting is not firm. A no-show charge is reimbursable only if the customer cancelled inside 24 hours or a documented emergency occurred.

Loyalty points are yours. Do not choose a hotel $40 over cap to collect a night toward status.

## 6. Meals and incidentals

Harborline uses a **daily meal cap**, not a federal per-diem pay-out. You expense actual meals up to the cap.

| Situation | Daily cap | Alcohol |
| --- | --- | --- |
| US business travel | **$75** | Max 2 drinks with a meal; no alcohol on the Company card at a bar without food |
| International business travel | **$100** | Same alcohol rule |
| Day trip (out and back, no hotel) | **$40** | Lunch only, no dinner unless you return after 8:00 p.m. local |
| Working lunch in a hub city (no travel) | **$25** per person | Team lunches need a roster in the Voyage comment |

The daily cap resets at midnight local time at the destination. A late-night arrival plus a hotel breakfast still shares that calendar day.

Itemize the tip. Tips up to 20% (US) or local custom (Ireland/EU) are allowed inside the cap. Grocery food for a week-long on-site is allowed and often cheaper; keep itemized receipts and do not include household items.

Meals with customers are **entertainment**, not the personal daily cap. See Section 8.

Meals at your home city are not travel meals. The bagel on the way to the Seattle office is personal.

## 7. Receipts, missing receipts, and small amounts

- Receipts are **required for every expense of $25 or more**.
- Below $25, a receipt is still preferred. If you lost it, itemize the merchant, amount, date, and attendees in Voyage.
- Itemized receipts are required for any meal of $25 or more. A credit-card total without items is not enough.
- Photographs of paper receipts are acceptable. The image must show merchant, date, amount, and last four card digits when printed.
- Missing-receipt affidavits are limited to **two per employee per quarter**. Finance will reject a third.

Do not redact customer names if the meal was client entertainment; Finance needs them for audit. Do redact personal loyalty numbers if you wish.

## 8. Customer and vendor entertainment

Taking a customer to dinner is allowed when there is a business purpose (deal, renewal, implementation, or executive relationship).

| Rule | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| Per-person meal including tax and tip | **$150** unless VP of Sales or VP of Customer Success pre-approves |
| Alcohol | Reasonable with the meal; no separate nightclub tab |
| Gifts to customers | **$75** fair-market value per person per year, and never cash or gift cards above $25 |
| Vendor-paid meals | Allowed if they do not influence a pending RFP. Disclose in the HarborHub gifts log if over $75. See **POL-CON-010** |
| Adult entertainment, gambling, political fundraisers | Never reimbursable |

Government customers (US federal, state, or local, and EU public bodies) may have stricter gift and meal rules. Follow the **stricter** of this policy and the customer’s ethics rules. If you do not know the customer’s rule, ask Legal before you book the restaurant.

## 9. Corporate cards

Directors and above, plus Sales and Customer Success employees who travel more than six nights a quarter, receive a Harborline corporate card. Cardholders:

- Use the card for policy-compliant business spend only.
- Do not use the card for personal expenses. Accidental personal charges must be reimbursed to Harborline within 15 days.
- Code every transaction in Voyage within 15 days.
- Do not withdraw cash except in a documented emergency (lost wallet abroad). Cash withdrawals require a HarborHub case within 48 hours.

Employees without a card use their own funds or request a pre-travel advance of up to $500 for international trips. Advances are deducted from the first Voyage report after return.

Late coding (more than 30 days) twice in a year can result in card suspension.

## 10. What we do not reimburse

The following are personal, even during a business trip:

- Airline club memberships (except a single day-pass under Section 4)
- TSA PreCheck or Global Entry after Harborline has already paid once (the Company pays one enrollment every five years)
- Childcare, pet boarding, and house-sitting
- Clothing, laundry (except after 6 consecutive hotel nights or a spilled-bag incident)
- Movies, minibar snacks, spa services, and in-room entertainment
- Traffic and parking tickets (even in a rental)
- Lost personal property, except as the travel insurance policy may cover
- Commuting, home internet beyond the stipend in **POL-RMT-003**, and home office furniture beyond the $500 setup cap
- Political contributions and charitable donations unless the CEO’s office organized a Company match
- Travel insurance you buy yourself (Harborline has a corporate policy; request a certificate from travel@harborline.example)

## 11. Combining personal travel

You may add personal days to a business trip if:

1. The business purpose is real and would have happened anyway.
2. You book the business itinerary first in HarborTravel, then change dates. You pay any fare difference caused by the personal legs.
3. Hotel and meals on personal days are not on Voyage.
4. The trip does not violate the international-work cap in **POL-RMT-003**.

Example: a Tuesday–Wednesday customer meeting in Chicago. You stay Saturday. Harborline pays the midweek fare and two hotel nights. You pay Friday and Saturday hotel and any extra air cost to return Sunday.

## 12. Spouses, partners, and team events

Companion travel is not reimbursed. If a companion shares a hotel room, expense only the single-occupancy rate (ask the hotel for a letter or use the HarborTravel single rate).

Team offsites have a separate budget owned by the function VP. Meals at offsites still follow the $75 / $100 caps unless the VP of Finance approves a packaged venue.

## 13. Health, safety, and duty of care

HarborTravel feeds itineraries to our duty-of-care vendor. Keep your mobile number current in HarborHub so we can reach you during an airline disruption or a regional emergency.

Do not book countries on the Harborline restricted-travel list (maintained by Legal and Security, posted in HarborHub). Medical evacuations and political unrest are why we have a corporate travel-insurance policy; personal adventure sports during a business trip may be excluded. Ask travel@harborline.example before a weekend climb or dive.

If you become ill on the road, get care, keep receipts, and notify hr@harborline.example. Medical expenses may be reimbursable after insurance; they are not a Voyage “meal.”

## 14. Examples

**Q. My Chicago hotel is $248 including tax. The cap is $225. What do I do?**  
A. Re-search HarborTravel for a policy rate. If nothing exists within 0.5 miles of the customer, file a HarborHub exception **before** you book. Do not book $248 and hope.

**Q. Lunch was $18. Do I need a receipt?**  
A. Not required, but attach one if you have it. At $25 the receipt becomes mandatory.

**Q. Can I expense a $32 dinner in Seattle the night before an early Austin flight?**  
A. If you had to stay near the airport because of a departure before 8:00 a.m., hotel and that dinner are travel. A routine dinner at home the night before a 10:00 a.m. flight is not.

**Q. A customer bought a $120 dinner for me. Do I report it?**  
A. Yes, in the gifts log if the value is over $75. You do not expense it. See **POL-CON-010**.

**Q. May I buy a second monitor on the corporate card while traveling?**  
A. No. Equipment goes through **POL-EQP-008** and IT, not Voyage travel categories.

## 15. Audits and consequences

Finance samples 10% of reports each month and 100% of reports over $2,500. Employees who submit falsified receipts, duplicate claims, or personal charges they refuse to repay are referred to People Operations. Outcomes range from repayment and coaching to termination.
